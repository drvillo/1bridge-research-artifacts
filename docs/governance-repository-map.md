The app is independent of observer availability: release and recovery operations do not call or wait for the observer. The shared observer polls on its own schedule and catches up from its retained accepted head. Enterprise/Government “strict” requires a customer-held action receipt and applies a 24-hour report freshness threshold at review time (`webapp/src/lib/authority/observer-review-policy.ts:9-21` on the app branch); it is not a release gate or a deployed retention SLA. A future customer-run Enterprise observer can watch its own feed; it is not a prerequisite for the current shared profile.

The four manuscript tracks have separate jobs: (1) encryption-system description of primitives and confidentiality boundaries, (2) system companion of product journeys and trust boundaries, (3) protocol description of authenticated artifacts and adversarial states, and (4) benchmark report of pinned actual implementations and raw measurements. The private `.tex` sources remain in the application's research worktree pending their final update. The experimental encrypted-intake study is supporting material; it is not a substitute for a deployed guest protocol.

Record six distinct evidence classes before a strong manuscript claim: library vectors, observer persistence/catch-up, app route/transaction behavior, cross-repository HTTP and adversarial cases, deployed source/observer observation, and reproducible performance samples. A signed provider checkpoint does not prove customer approval; a customer signature does not prove recipient delivery; a Merkle inclusion proof does not prove completeness of actions that nobody observed.
